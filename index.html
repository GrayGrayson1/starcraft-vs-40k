Three Wars, Two Universes

Both settings run three-way wars between a human military empire, a devouring biological swarm and an ancient psychic civilisation in decline. The mapping below is the frame for everything that follows.

The thing everyone repeats — and what actually happened

The story that StarCraft began life as a licensed Warhammer 40,000 game is folklore. The licensing conversation was real, but it was about Warcraft, not StarCraft. Blizzard co-founder Allen Adham pursued a Warhammer licence for the first Warcraft; producer Patrick Wyatt's own account says it died from "a lack of traction on business terms and a fervent desire on the part of virtually everyone else on the development team … to control our own universe."

What did happen to StarCraft is more interesting. Its 1996 E3 build — running on the Warcraft II engine — was dismissed as "orcs in space," and Blizzard threw the engine out and rebuilt the game over roughly two more years before its 1998 release. The resemblances collected on this page are mostly convergent design: two teams solving the same problems — a horde, an elite, a line infantryman, an artillery piece — and arriving at the same answers.

Method & Sources

How the numbers were made

  1. StarCraft II stats are extracted from the game's own balance data — health, shields, armour, supply, cost, build time, movement speed, sight, attack damage, cooldown and range. Damage per second is damage × attacks ÷ cooldown. Nothing is hand-entered.
  2. Warhammer 40,000 stats are read from current 10th-edition datasheets: Movement, Toughness, Save, Wounds, Leadership, Objective Control, weapon range, attacks, skill, Strength, AP and Damage, plus the published base size.
  3. The profile chart shows each unit's percentile rank inside its own game's roster — not raw values. A 40K Wound and a StarCraft hit point are not the same thing and are never plotted on one scale. Durability is HP + shields on one side, Wounds × Toughness on the other; damage volume is DPS against expected attacks × damage; reach, mobility and footprint use each game's own units.
  4. Where a value doesn't exist — a StarCraft attack driven by an ability rather than a weapon, a 40K vehicle with no base size — the chart prints "no data in source" instead of a zero.
  5. The convergence score is editorial judgement, scored on a fixed four-part rubric and always shown broken down. It is the only subjective number here.
